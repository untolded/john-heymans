## What we're building

A new personal-brand website for **John Heymans**, Belgian Olympic 5000m finalist and keynote speaker. Keynote-led, but it is his brand home, not a product page for one talk.

**Primary job:** convert corporate decision-makers into booking enquiries.
**Underlying job:** make a premium fee feel obviously correct. Judge every decision against that.

It replaces a Wix template that is actively costing him credibility.

---

## The hook — get this right or nothing else matters

Two years from deciding to try, to the Olympic final. He got there by building an **AI algorithm that selected the optimal competition schedule to maximise his world ranking**, against the advice of his federation, his coach, and his competitors. It produced the fastest rise up the world rankings in his sport's history.

That is the whole proposition: a strategy-and-risk story that happens to take place on a running track. He is not an athlete telling a room to believe in itself.

Anchor line, written on his arm at the Games: **"Hey mom, made it."** The most ownable asset in this brand. Use it, but earn it — don't staple it on as a tagline.

## Tone of voice

Write like him, not like a speaker bureau. Direct, first-person, understated about the achievement, specific about the method. Confident without volume. Slightly dry. The facts are extraordinary enough that they don't need adjectives helping them.

**Do**
- First person. "I had no support for this" beats "John had no support for this."
- Short declarative sentences. Let the numbers carry it: two years, 5000m, Olympic final, fastest rise in the sport's history.
- Be concrete about the method. Name the tool, name the risk, name who told him not to.
- CTAs that say what happens: "Check John's availability," "Watch the 60-second film."
- Sentence case throughout.

**Don't**
- Speaker-bureau language. Banned: *inspiring, unique energy, authenticity, captivated the audience, powerful lessons, takes you on a journey*.
- Generic motivational register. He is explicitly not an American motivational speaker. "Dare to dream big" is a line from his keynote in his own voice — it is not body copy and not a headline.
- Third person about himself in body copy.
- Corporate filler: leverage, synergies, transformative.
- Em-dash-heavy rhythm and "not X, but Y" constructions. They read as AI-written and the client notices.
- Exclamation marks. The old site uses them in CTAs. Stop.
- Never use em-dashes

**Language:** English-first. Keep all copy i18n-ready — no strings hardcoded into layout, allow for Dutch running 15–20% longer. **Do not build a language switcher yet**; the setup is undecided.

## Design direction

You have full creative freedom on typography, colour, composition, layout system, motion, art direction and section order.

**Non-negotiable**
- Must feel expensive and fast. If it reads as a template, it has failed.
- The hero lands the hook without scrolling.
- Both the **promo film** and a **booking enquiry modal** live in the hero. You decide which is primary; make it a deliberate, stated choice.
- Quality floor: responsive to mobile, visible keyboard focus, reduced motion respected, accessible contrast.
- Photo credits are preserved wherever images appear (Jelle Jansegers, Raf Thomas, others).

**Do not reach for these — they are defaults, not decisions**
Cream background + high-contrast serif + terracotta accent. Near-black + one acid-green or vermilion accent. Broadsheet hairline rules and dense columns. Identical rounded cards with the same soft grey shadow. Tracked-out all-caps eyebrows above every heading. Middle-dot meta strings. Monospace micro-labels. Arrows appended to button text. Fade-and-slide-up on every section.

**Spend the freedom on this subject instead.** Athletics has its own visual vernacular: lane geometry, split times, pace curves, altitude, the training log, the world-ranking table, the gap between an algorithm's output and a body's limit. That is where anything distinctive will come from.

Spend boldness in one place. One memorable element; everything around it quiet and disciplined.

## Process

Before building anything visual, write a short plan — colour tokens (4–6 named hex values), typefaces and their roles, layout concept, and the principles that make this page specific to John. Then check it against this file: if any part is what you'd produce for any speaker's website, revise it and say what changed and why.

Real copy only. No lorem ipsum, no placeholder headlines. Write as if shipping.
