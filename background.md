## 1. Who he is

**John Heymans.** Belgian middle/long-distance runner. **Olympic 5000m finalist.** Keynote speaker.

- Bio-engineering student, graduated during Covid. Started running seriously only in his late teens.
- The Olympic ambition did not exist until **two years** before the Games. He decided on it, then went after it.
- Made the Olympic 5000m final, which almost nobody believed was possible.
- Written on his arm at the Games: **"Hey mom, made it."**

He has delivered roughly **15 keynotes** to date, entirely through word of mouth, with consistently strong feedback. The keynote is **30 minutes excluding Q&A**, with optional Q&A, available in **English, Dutch or French**.

He is explicitly positioning to charge more and to stop relying on referrals.

## 2. The keynote — full structure

His framing: the story of his journey to the Olympic Games in five parts, each with lessons applicable in business, *especially for organisations coming off a difficult year*.

A supporting PowerPoint exists (shared by Dropbox). The slides mean little without the talk.

### Part 1 — Kenya: environment and consistency

Shortly after graduating he impulsively booked a flight to **Iten, Kenya** — a village at **2,400m altitude** where the best African athletes train.

> *"You are the average of the 5 people you spend the most time with."*

Being surrounded by people who are better than you and share your mindset. We're products of our environment; professionals grow fastest in teams that set high standards.

> *"Consistency beats hard work."*

Africa's best don't win by training exceptionally hard for a few days. They deliver quality every day, 365 days a year. Daily discipline outperforms spectacular days.

### Part 2 — AI and ChatGPT: challenging the status quo

**This is the differentiator. Treat it as the centre of the story.**

Because everyone at the top already trains consistently, he needed an edge. He built an **AI algorithm that identified the optimal competitions to enter to maximise his world ranking** and secure Olympic qualification.

His competition choices went **directly against the advice of his federation, his coach, and his fellow competitors.** It was a real risk. It worked: the **fastest rise up the world rankings in his sport's history**, and Olympic qualification.

> *"Don't be afraid to challenge the status quo. It's where competitive advantage is found."*

Organisations that question standard processes and inherited assumptions are the ones that find new opportunities and durable advantage.

### Part 3 — Failures: dealing with setbacks

He is candid about his setbacks. In any situation there are controllables — sleep, nutrition, training — and uncontrollables — illness, weather, injury. Business equivalents: you control preparation, collaboration and execution; you don't control macro shifts, market volatility or external disruption.

> *"Focus on the controllable. Don't overthink what you can't control."*

After a setback, work out which contributing factors were actually within your control, and spend energy only there.

### Part 4 — Olympic Village: performing under pressure

A behind-the-scenes look at the Olympic Village. He saw two kinds of athlete: those overwhelmed by stress, and those who enjoyed the experience regardless of external pressure. He chose to be the second kind.

> *"Embrace stressful situations."*

High-pressure moments usually coincide with the important opportunities — leading a transformation, making a critical strategic call. They're rare, which is exactly why they're worth experiencing fully rather than buckling under.

### Part 5 — Olympic final: dreaming big

He closes on the final itself.

> *"Dare to dream big."*

Dream small and you never give yourself the means to do something great. Your dreams determine your ceiling. Organisations and individuals who set ambitious goals are far likelier to unlock exceptional performance.

## 3. Audience

In priority order:

1. **The event owner / decision-maker.** Conference organiser, head of HR or L&D, marketing lead, executive assistant booking a board offsite. Usually comparing three or four speakers. Needs to know quickly: credible, interesting, safe choice, how to get a price.
2. **The senior leader who already saw him speak** and is considering bringing him into their own company. Already sold — needs a frictionless path to enquiry.
3. **Press and general public.** Lower priority; the site just shouldn't embarrass him.

Real booking contexts, from his own material:

- Global business fair or conference, 1,000+ delegates
- Employee team-building event, 100+ employees
- Executive strategy offsite, 20–50 senior leaders
- Networking dinner, 8–20 senior leaders

The range is deliberately wide. Don't narrow the site to one segment, but make it obvious he's as comfortable in a boardroom as in a stadium.

## 7. Audit of the current site

`johnheymans.com` — Wix template. What's wrong, and what the rebuild has to fix:

- **No identity.** No typographic voice, no colour system, no point of view. Could be any consultant. Nothing signals Olympian, or AI, or risk.
- **The hook is buried.** Above the fold: a cropped banner and a "They booked me" logo row. The two-year arc and the AI algorithm — the only two things that justify a premium fee — appear nowhere at first glance.
- **Third-person brochure copy.** "In recent years, John has inspired audiences…"
- **The AI story is undersold.** The site says he used ChatGPT and AI tools "to gain a competitive edge." The actual story — an algorithm to game the world-ranking system, against his federation's advice, producing a record rise — is far stronger and isn't told.
- **Proof badly presented.** Two excellent testimonials as unbroken text walls. Logos at mismatched sizes, some captioned "Best beer brewery in the world" instead of named.
- **CTAs go nowhere.** "Book now," "Secure John for your next event!" and "Send me a more detailed summary" all link back to the same page.
- **Credibility leaks in the footer.** "©2025 by coach John Heymans" — he isn't positioned as a coach here — and social icons pointing at Wix's own Facebook, Twitter and LinkedIn accounts.
- **No anchoring.** Nothing helps a buyer place what tier of speaker this is.
